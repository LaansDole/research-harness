1 · A scoping review, by the repo's own runbook

This capture is docs/RUNBOOK.md performed live: both Part 0 environment gates, then Part 1 as far as the first screening pass. A researcher opens with one plain sentence — "I'm starting a scoping review on whether teams of LLM agents that talk to each other make hospital diagnosis better than a single model. Where do I start?" — then imports a real 50-record PubMed export and works it down to a PRISMA diagram. Every number on screen is computed during the take, out of the review store as it fills up.

13 min 24 s · 1600x1000 · eleven typed lines, unedited, trimmed only at the tail. Each pause is sized to that turn's measured headless runtime, so a finished turn does not stretch the video — but the agent's real work and your own reading time are still on screen. Scrub the player rather than watching every second.

  • Part 0 — research doctor and the 109-test suite, the two gates the runbook makes you pass before starting.
  • Scope — the question in plain words becomes a PCC framing and a written scope.md, with the inclusion gate the reviewer will be held to.
  • /help — the capability guide: every command, what it does, when you would reach for it. The bar at the bottom tracks the active project and screening progress all the way through.
  • Import + dedupe — 50 PubMed records in, 0 duplicate(s) marked out (correct for a single-database export).
  • The abstract problem — record 1 shows what a PubMed CSV does not carry, and the fix: OpenAlex lookups that bring the abstracts back.
  • Screening — five records judged against the researcher's own criteria, each with an evidence sentence, a first-failed dimension and a confidence; one left as maybe for full text, and a genuine tension between scope.md and SCREENING.md raised rather than papered over. Nothing is written until the researcher confirms.
  • /prisma — the flow diagram derived from record state, with the arithmetic line that has to balance.

2 · The librarian agent grows the paper graph

The same plain-language posture, aimed at the second brain: "here's my folder of papers on multi-agent medical AI — add the most relevant ones to my paper graph and tell me what connects them", then "export that view so I can explore it myself".

1600x1000 · 1x playback. The corpus is a six-paper folder for this topic; the real library is larger and the full scan does not fit a capture.

3 · The review in your browser: research web

One command opens the agent in your browser on 127.0.0.1, beside everything it has written for the project. This take walks the demo-web-ui review: a starter command from the empty chat, the reading list filtered by state, searched, and triaged from the keyboard with each record's verdicts, rationale and abstract in the side panel; the review's contents and a citation jumping to its reference; a paper found in the graph and handed to the agent; and the PRISMA diagram the counts come from.

53 s · 1440x900 · recorded live from Chrome with Puppeteer's page.screencast() (script), so the take renders in its own running time. Nothing is sent: every action button only fills the chat box, and nothing runs until the researcher presses Enter — the agent stays the only writer of the review.

  • Header — the project you are viewing beside the one the agent works on, a Make active button, the agent's status and model.
  • Reading list — state chips with counts, search across title, author and DOI, ↑/↓ to move, Enter to open, Esc to close; a next-step bar such as 1 full text ready · Screen full texts.
  • Reviews — a contents list, [n] citations that preview on hover and jump to their reference, and Revise / Check per section.
  • Graph — search a paper and press Enter; Find citing papers, Auto-edges and Explain neighbors prefill the agent.
  • PRISMA — the flow diagram derived from record state, with SVG download.

4 · The exported paper graph

One self-contained HTML file: vanilla-JS force-directed canvas, node size by degree, edge colours by type, search, drag/zoom/pan, click for metadata. No external assets — it works from file://.

Why these live here: GitHub refuses to render repository blobs larger than about 1 MB in its file view and serves repository blobs as application/octet-stream, so a committed MP4 cannot be watched in place. GitHub Pages serves them as real video/mp4, so they play here at full quality.
