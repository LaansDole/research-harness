A researcher picks up her scoping review

She is reviewing how teams of LLM agents support clinical decisions. One command, research web, opens the agent in her browser on her own machine (127.0.0.1). In the take she asks where the review stands, opens her reading list from the PRISMA strip, has the words from her own criteria marked, screens four papers herself (include, exclude with a reason, maybe, and one the agent had already screened), marks a claim in her review to check, glances at the paper graph and ends on the PRISMA diagram her decisions just changed, then on a phone-width screen.

Chapters 14

How it was recorded

5 min 38 s, recorded live from headless Chrome with Puppeteer's page.screencast() (script) at 1440x900 and shown at 1x. The phone-width part is a second recording at 390x900, letterboxed and joined after the first; that join is the only cut. research web and its agent ran with a scratch home folder holding a copy of the project, so nothing in the real research folder changed.

What is real, and what was added

One prompt is sent and answered for real: Where does my review stand? The claim check is only written into the message box and never sent. Every decision at the desk is saved to the project's review store, the same one the agent writes to. For the take, the scratch copy of the project gained exclusion and inclusion criteria and six more real papers from OpenAlex, so there was something left to screen.

What's on screen

Every part of research web the take passes through.

Header and PRISMA strip
Across the top runs a small PRISMA flow: Identified, Screened, Sought, Assessed and Included, each with its count and what is still waiting under it (5 to screen). Clicking a box opens the reading list on those papers. The tabs are Chat, Graph, Reviews, Reading list and PRISMA, with a sun/moon button for dark mode.
Chat
The empty chat offers next steps in plain words instead of commands. She asks Where does my review stand?; the status turns Working…, the agent's steps stay folded under thinking · N steps, and the answer is a table of where the review stands, matching the strip.
Highlight words
Highlight words opens two lists, include and exclude. Suggest from your criteria fills them from her scope, she removes a word she disagrees with and saves. From then on those words are marked in every title and abstract: green for include, vermilion and underlined for exclude, with a legend above the abstract.
Screening desk
An open paper shows Include, Exclude and Maybe with their keys I, E and M. She includes one with I, and the panel moves to the next paper while the strip counts down. She excludes one with E, picks which of her criteria it failed from a numbered list and adds a note. She marks one Maybe, which keeps it waiting. On a paper the agent already screened, the desk shows its verdict, reason and confidence, and she confirms it.
Reading list
Each paper's verdict carries a mark: green with a check for include, vermilion with a cross for exclude, yellow with a question mark for maybe. Filters by stage, a search box, and each row says what it needs next.
Reviews
She selects a sentence in her review and a note appears in the margin beside it, quoting it, with Check claim. Pressing it writes the request into the message box and keeps the sentence tinted; nothing is sent.
Paper graph
How her papers connect: cites, related and same-topic links.
Dark mode and PRISMA
The sun/moon button turns the whole app to a calm charcoal. The PRISMA diagram is drawn from her records, so her own exclusion reason now has its own line in the excluded box, and the papers still waiting are counted below.
Phone width
At 390 px the strip collapses to one line (18 screened · 8 sought · 4 included), and a paper opens as a full-screen sheet with its marked abstract.

The exported paper graph

The Graph tab shows the paper graph's own export: one self-contained HTML file that draws the papers as a network you can drag, zoom, pan and search. Bigger dots have more links, line colours show the link type, and clicking a paper shows its details. It loads nothing from the internet, so it also opens straight from disk.
