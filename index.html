research-harness — the review, in your browser

An unedited demo of research-harness's browser UI, research web, over a real scoping review. Real data, real views, no staged replay — and nothing is sent to the agent: every action button only fills the chat box.

1440x900 · 1x playback · light mode · open-access sources only

1 · A researcher picks up her scoping review

She is reviewing how teams of LLM agents support clinical decisions. One command opens the agent in her browser on 127.0.0.1, beside everything it has written for the review: she checks where things stand, triages the reading list, reads the synthesis and checks one claim against its sources, explores how her papers connect in the graph, and ends on the PRISMA diagram for her methods section.

1 min 40 s · recorded live from Chrome with Puppeteer's page.screencast() (script), so a take renders in its own running time. Nothing runs until the researcher presses Enter, so the agent stays the only writer of the review.

  • Where was I? — the header shows the review she is viewing beside the one the agent works on, the agent's status and model; the empty chat offers the next commands. Make active fills in the switch, and Restore previous draft brings back the plan she had half-typed.
  • Her workspace — projects with screening progress, recent sessions, model, thinking level and appearance in the sidebar; Ctrl/⌘+B folds it away.
  • Reading list — a next-step bar (1 full text ready · Screen full texts), state chips with counts, the five title/abstract exclusions with their rationale, ↑/↓ through records, search, and an included paper's verdicts, abstract and PDF.
  • The review — jump by contents, follow a [n] citation to its reference and back, then select one sentence and Check claim hands it to the agent with the file and project named.
  • Paper graph — hovering a paper labels it and its neighbours; clicking TriageAgent opens its record and the agent actions; zoom, clear, search galatea + Enter, and Find citing papers for TriageAgent.
  • PRISMA — the flow diagram derived from record state, every number counted from the review store, with SVG download.

2 · The exported paper graph

One self-contained HTML file: vanilla-JS force-directed canvas, node size by degree, edge colours by type, search, drag/zoom/pan, click for metadata. No external assets — it works from file://.

Why the video lives here: GitHub refuses to render repository blobs larger than about 1 MB in its file view and serves repository blobs as application/octet-stream, so a committed MP4 cannot be watched in place. GitHub Pages serves it as real video/mp4, so it plays here at full quality.
